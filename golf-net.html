Golf Practice Net

10ft × 7ft × 3ft · PVC Frame · 3D-Printed ASA Joints · #21 Knotted Nylon Netting

CAD Design 3D Printing Structural Design Solo Project In Progress
01 — Overview

Why Build It?

Commercial golf practice nets are expensive and use generic hardware-store fittings that aren't optimized for the geometry of a practice net. This project is a ground-up design — every joint, connection point, and structural member was modeled in SolidWorks before any material was purchased, ensuring the frame handles repeated impact loading from full golf swings without racking or joint failure.

The net is designed for indoor use as part of a home simulator setup, with a 10ft × 7ft face and 3ft depth to safely catch shots at full swing speed. The angled rear supports transfer impact loads efficiently to the base rather than relying purely on joint friction.

02 — Design

Frame & Joints

The frame uses 1-1/4" Schedule 40 PVC pipe throughout. Standard PVC fittings don't come in the exact angles needed for a freestanding net with angled rear braces, so all structural joints were custom designed in SolidWorks and 3D-printed in ASA filament — chosen specifically for its UV and heat resistance over standard PLA or PETG, important for a fixture that may see outdoor or garage use.

Two joint types were designed: a standard 3-way corner joint for the top and bottom frame corners, and a 4-way joint with angled side port for the mid-frame nodes where the rear diagonal braces connect. The angled port geometry was modeled to match the exact brace angle needed to keep the net freestanding and stable under impact load.
