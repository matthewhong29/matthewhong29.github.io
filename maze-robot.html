01 — Overview

A Two-Person Build

Built October–December 2025 with my classmate Kyle Tran — a tank-drive robot that autonomously maps and solves a previously unseen maze. The system runs concurrent sensor polling, motor control, and real-time pathfinding on a single Raspberry Pi Pico (MicroPython + asyncio) without blocking the navigation loop, building an internal map of visited cells and backtracking to explore unvisited branches until it finds a way through.

The platform uses ultrasonic and IR sensors for wall/obstacle detection, a tank-drive dual-motor configuration, an I2C LCD with status LEDs for live feedback, and a bench potentiometer for tuning behavior without reflashing firmware.

02 — My Role

My Contributions

Kyle led the robot itself: he designed the full robot in CAD and wrote all of the MicroPython firmware. My part was the early concept sketches of the chassis and the test maze the robot had to solve. I laid the maze out and dimensioned it in AutoCAD and designed the 3D-printed corner brackets that join its walls. I also modeled a handful of smaller robot components while learning CAD from Kyle.

Full credit for the robot's CAD and firmware belongs to Kyle. His writeup is linked below.

03 — Technical Overview

How It Navigates

Sensing
  • Ultrasonic sensors for wall/obstacle distance
  • IR sensors for line-following and proximity
  • I2C LCD + status LEDs for live feedback
Control
  • Raspberry Pi Pico, MicroPython + asyncio
  • Concurrent sensing, motor control, and pathfinding
  • Bench potentiometer for tuning without reflashing
Navigation
  • Tank-drive dual-motor platform
  • Builds an internal map of visited cells
  • Backtracks to explore unvisited branches
